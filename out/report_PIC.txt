PORTFOLIO COMMENTARY
Market Commentary
The S&P/ASX 300 Accumulation Index fell 2.43% in September, snapping a five-month winning streak in the weakest month since
March. A global bond rout dominated, with Australian 10-year yields climbing to 15-year highs. Renewed US Iran tensions pushed
oil higher, adding to inflation concerns. The RBA raised the cash rate 25 basis points to 4.60%, its fourth hike this year, citing
persistent inflation, higher energy costs and weak productivity. Headline CPI rose to 4.0% in August, driven by fuel and electricity,
although trimmed mean inflation held at 3.6%. Q2 GDP beat expectations while unemployment rose to a five-year high. Healthcare
(+3.90%) led, supported by CSL, Ansell and Ramsay, with Energy (+0.49%) also positive. Industrials, Financials and Consumer
Staples outperformed. Rate-sensitive sectors lagged, with Information Technology (-9.84%) the weakest as WiseTech, Xero and
NextDC sold off, while Materials (-5.86%) fell on critical minerals weakness.
Portfolio
The portfolio's largest overweight positions include Washington H Soul Pattinson, News Corporation and Rio Tinto. Conversely, the
portfolio's largest relative underweight positions include CBA and NAB, both of which were not held, and BHP.
Contributors
Washington H. Soul Pattinson (SOL)
SOL was the top contributor in September, returning 7.37%. During the month SOL reported a solid FY26 result, its first full financial
year since the merger with Brickworks (BKW). The key positives for us were a pre-tax NAV return of 10.2% (approximately 5
percentage points ahead of the All Ords), an 11.5% increase in net cash flows from investments and a 7% increase in the final fully
franked dividend. The BKW merger has been strategically transformative for SOL. By resetting the tax cost base of SOL’s entire
portfolio to market value, it turned a $1.4 billion deferred tax liability into a $792 million deferred tax asset. This removed the capital
gains tax constraint on long-held legacy positions and gave management greater freedom to reposition the portfolio. This has
accelerated a longer-term shift in the listed/unlisted split from 90/10 at the end of FY21 to 40/60 at the end of FY26, with fixed
income, private companies and credit the main beneficiaries. The outcome is a more diversified portfolio generating greater
recurring cash flow, as reflected in this year’s 11.5% increase. This should continue to support SOL’s 28-year record of dividend
increases. Recent portfolio activity has left SOL with close to $3 billion of cash and short-term fixed income, a valuable source of
liquidity that can be used to fund higher returning opportunities as they emerge. In the current volatile environment, this liquidity
position combined with SOL’s disciplined capital allocation leaves the company well placed. The stock remains a core holding due
to the superior risk adjusted returns from its diversified but high-quality portfolio, which, in our view, is led by one of the best
management teams in the market.

Suncorp Group (SUN)
Suncorp returned 5.9% over the month, with the main news for the stock being reports on September 30 that Suncorp had entered
preliminary takeover talks with Tokio Marine Holdings, which saw shares jump as much as 7.5% intraday. Suncorp formally denied
the reports however, and shares fell 7.4% in the next session, unwinding the gain. Although the talks were denied, we see the
possible corporate interest in Suncorp being indicative of the quality of the business and the local industry generally. Following the
completion of the ANZ bank sale, Suncorp is now a pure-play Australian insurer, which represents a clean structural opportunity for
an offshore acquirer, making further corporate interest plausible. Separately, given the current market backdrop, Suncorp serves an
important defensive role in the portfolio and is now one of the larger active weights in the PIC. On top of the defensive nature of
home and motor insurance, Suncorp is benefitting from the higher interest rate environment as it earns investment income on the
cash it receives upfront from customers.
Detractors
Aspen Group (APZ)
APZ returned -12.1% during September amid continued negative sentiment surrounding the property sector, driven by the prospect
of further interest rate increases and weakening consumer sentiment. This is reflected in the share price decline in FY27 being
primarily driven by multiple compression, with consensus forward earnings per share upgraded over the period. Importantly for APZ,
affordable housing has historically proven more resilient at the demand level during economic slowdowns, supported by the
essential and often counter-cyclical need for accommodation and structurally low vacancy rates, in contrast to more cyclical
segments such as luxury residential, office and hospitality. This view was supported by a Q1 update on October 5 which illustrated
strong operating momentum and no signs of weakness from broader deterioration in property prices. Management also reaffirmed
FY27 guidance, which we believe appears conservative. For these reasons, APZ remains a top five active weight in the portfolio.
Pinnacle Investment Management Group (PNI)
PNI fell 21.3% over the month, largely due to delays in auditing several listed investment trusts (LITs) managed by Metrics Credit
Partners, an affiliate in which PNI holds a 35% stake. On 31 August, Metrics published unaudited FY26 accounts for Metrics Master
Income Trust (MXT), Metrics Income Opportunities Trust (MOT) andMetrics Real Estate Multi-Strategy Fund (MRE), creating
uncertainty about their accuracy and contributing to share price declines of 10.1% and 5.8% over the next two tradingsessions. Two
days before the 30 September reporting deadline, Metrics announced NTA write-downs of 2% for MXT, 10% for MOTand 12% for
MRE. Audited financial statements were released on 2 October, ending a month-long standoff with KPMG. While this isclearly
negative for Metrics’ growth and profitability, Metrics is only one of Pinnacle’s many affiliate partners and investments. Beforethese
developments, Metrics was estimated to contribute approximately 10–12% of PNI’s NPAT. In our view, Pinnacle’s share price
correction therefore more than reflects the full value of Metrics. The focus on Metrics has overshadowed strong progress elsewhere
in PNI’s affiliate base, including continued growth in its international platform and strong flows into Lifecycle, Coolabah and Plato.
Outlook
Inflation remains a significant focus for markets, with ongoing government spending, the Middle East conflict and the AI build-out the
key forces. At this stage, these drivers are not sensitive to rates, leaving monetary policy less effective at bringing inflation under
control in the near term. Amid these pressures, the Australian consumer and economy continue to soften, weighed down by a
higher cost of living, falling asset prices (particularly property, on the back of Government policy) and weak confidence. We find it
hard to be overly positive on an improvement in the Australian consumer unless we see a resolution in the Middle East or a
reduction in the AI build-out’s demand for capital. Against this backdrop, and with cost inflation still elevated, the outlook for
Australian corporates remains tough. We believe quality companies with strong balance sheets and management teams are best
placed to take market share and outperform in this environment. This is reflected in our portfolio positioning.
